What is this showing? +

This is a browser-side demonstration of multi-criteria decision-making, not a process simulator. The 91 synthetic designs deliberately trade higher carbon utilisation against higher capital or electricity indices. They are not extracted from a paper and cannot support an engineering conclusion.

TOPSIS uses Euclidean vector normalisation, user-normalised weights, a benefit direction for carbon utilisation and cost directions for electricity and capital. The selected design maximises Cᵢ = Dᵢ⁻ / (Dᵢ⁺ + Dᵢ⁻). Changing a weight reranks this fixed example dataset; it does not rerun an optimiser.

Ties are resolved by design ID. Displayed weights are rounded to a total of 100%; calculations use unrounded values. The CSV includes the full dataset, weights, ranks and closeness scores.

04 / A LITTLE ABOUT ME

An engineer.
A systems thinker.

I’m Yuanjing (Jason) Zhao, a PhD researcher in chemical engineering at the University of Surrey.

I work at the intersection of electrochemical systems, sustainable chemical production and data-driven modelling. I’m interested in how the details of a technology change the bigger picture — and how better models can make those decisions clearer.
